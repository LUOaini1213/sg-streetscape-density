Pairwise perception survey: pre-registration-style protocol (draft v0.1)

Status: not run. No responses have been collected from anyone. Everything below that looks like a result comes from simulated respondents. The survey page in this repository is a demonstration that stores nothing and sends nothing. Independent personal project, not affiliated with the Singapore-ETH Centre or the NUS Urban Analytics Lab.

1. Question

At a given built density, do street scenes with more visible greenery (green view index, GVI) or more visible sky (sky view index, SVI) feel less crowded? If yes, greenery and sky openness are candidate design levers for “softening” density, which is what the street-level image analysis in this repository (README, Tier A and Tier B) can only show as association.

2. Hypotheses (directional, stated before any data)

  • H1 (primary). Controlling for the scene’s density proxy, higher GVI raises the probability of a scene being chosen as “feels less crowded” (β_GVI > 0 in the second-stage regression, one-sided α = 0.05 reported as two-sided p < 0.05 with the predicted sign).
  • H2 (secondary). Controlling for density, higher SVI raises that probability (β_SVI > 0).
  • H3 (secondary). Higher density proxy (log(1 + GFA intensity) of the scene’s 250 m cell) lowers it (β_density < 0).
  • Exploratory, not confirmatory: interactions GVI × density, and agreement of the human ranking with the Place Pulse-style perception predictions shipped in Global Streetscapes.

3. Stimuli

  • 40 scenes from the island-wide KartaView sample (CC BY-SA 4.0), only photos that KartaView’s own pipeline reports as face/plate-blurred, stratified into 4 density quartiles × 3 GVI tertiles so that GVI and density are not collinear in the stimulus set (scripts/10_survey_design.py, docs/survey/stimuli.json).
  • GVI/SVI of each stimulus come from our Mask2Former re-segmentation, which reproduces the Global Streetscapes values on 477 overlapping images (README).
  • Known limitations of the stimulus set: dashcam viewpoint (road, not pavement), mixed capture years (2015–2026), weather and time of day vary.

4. Task and design

  • Two-alternative forced choice: “Which street feels less crowded?”
  • Each respondent sees 20 pairs: 19 random distinct pairs plus one repeated pair with sides swapped (within-respondent consistency check). Left/right position randomised.
  • No identifying data: no names, e-mails or IP logging; optional coarse demographics (age band, years lived in Singapore, housing type) only in a real deployment, after ethics review.

5. Sample size (simulation-based power)

Generative model for the simulation: latent utility of scene i = β·z(GVI_i) − 0.3·z(density_i) + u_i, with u_i ~ N(0, σ²) for scene-level appeal not explained by GVI or density (σ = 0.5 or 1.0), rater discrimination 1 + N(0, 0.3²), Bradley–Terry choice probabilities, 200 simulated studies per cell. The analysis replicated in each simulated study is exactly the planned one (§6). The scene-level term u_i means that power is capped by the number of scenes, not only by the number of respondents.

What the table shows. Power barely grows beyond 25–50 respondents. With 40 scenes, the scene-level term u_i, which every respondent shares, is the binding constraint, not respondent noise. A small effect (β = 0.1) is never reliably detectable with this stimulus set. At σ = 1.0, even β = 0.35 is roughly a coin flip.

Design decision (planned). The lever is the number of scenes.

  • Before any confirmatory run, enlarge the stimulus set (each respondent still sees 20 random pairs from it) and re-run this simulation for the enlarged set. We have not yet simulated larger sets; that is the next step.
  • A small pilot (about 20 respondents) would be used only to estimate σ, not to test H1–H3.
  • The confirmatory sample size is then fixed in advance from the updated table, and the data are analysed once.

6. Analysis plan

  1. Exclusions (decided now): respondents with median response time < 400 ms or who choose the same side in ≥ 18 of 20 pairs. The repeated pair is reported, not used for exclusion.
  2. Stage 1: Bradley–Terry scores per scene, pooled over respondents (Hunter’s MM algorithm with 0.5 virtual wins/losses against a reference, ssd/pairwise.py). TrueSkill scores computed as a robustness check.
  3. Stage 2: OLS of the 40 scene scores on z(GVI), z(SVI) and z(density). Inference at the scene level (n = 40), which is conservative with respect to the respondent count. H1 is supported if β_GVI > 0 with p < 0.05.
  4. Robustness: (a) Bradley–Terry logit with scene covariates and a respondent random effect; (b) leave-one-planning-area-out on scenes;
    1. excluding scenes with rain or night lighting.

7. Ethics

  • A real deployment requires review by an institutional review board before any recruitment, an information sheet, and an explicit consent step. Adults only.
  • Responses would be anonymous; only choice data and coarse optional demographics would be kept; no free text.
  • Images: KartaView photos under CC BY-SA 4.0, credited per image; only photos the platform reports as blurred are shown. Scenes may still contain identifiable buildings and vehicles; respondents are not asked about people.
  • The demo page in this repository does not collect or transmit anything.
